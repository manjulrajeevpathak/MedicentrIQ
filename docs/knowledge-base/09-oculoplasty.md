# Oculoplasty — Eyelids, Tear Ducts, Sockets & Around-the-Eye Surgery

## Summary for prompt

Oculoplasty is the speciality for eyelids, tear drainage, the eye socket
and the area around the eye. Common problems: droopy eyelid (ptosis — in a
child it can block vision development and needs early review), stye/
chalazion (painful or painless lid lumps — warm compresses first, minor
procedure if persistent), constantly watering eyes (often a blocked tear
duct — cured by DCR surgery; in babies massage usually resolves it),
in-turned or out-turned lids, thyroid eye disease (bulging eyes), lid
tumours, and custom artificial eyes after loss of an eye. Watering with a
painful swelling near the nose-corner of the eye can be an infected tear
sac — treat as urgent.

## Droopy eyelid (ptosis)

Ptosis is drooping of the upper eyelid — from ageing of the lid muscle,
present since birth, after injury, or (less commonly) from nerve/muscle
disorders. In ADULTS it narrows the field of view, causes brow-ache and a
tired look; surgery tightens or supports the lifting muscle. In CHILDREN,
a lid covering the pupil can cause permanent lazy eye — congenital ptosis
needs early specialist assessment (surgery timing is individualised; vision
is monitored throughout childhood). Sudden ptosis with double vision,
headache or unequal pupils is an emergency neurological sign — same-day
evaluation.

## Stye and chalazion (lid lumps)

A **stye (guheri)** is a painful red boil at the lash line from a blocked,
infected gland — most settle in days with warm compresses (a clean, warm —
not hot — cloth for 5–10 minutes, 3–4 times daily) and prescribed drops or
ointment if needed. A **chalazion** is the painless firm lump that can
remain after inflammation; small ones often resolve over weeks with
compresses, persistent ones are cleared by a minor 10-minute procedure
from inside the lid (no visible scar). Do not squeeze or prick lid lumps.
Recurring lumps at the same spot in an older adult should be biopsied to
rule out a gland tumour; frequent styes also warrant checking for blood
sugar, dandruff-related lid margin disease and uncorrected power.

## Watering eyes and blocked tear ducts (DCR)

Tears drain through tiny openings at the inner lid corner into the nose; a
blocked drain makes the eye water constantly (worse outdoors/in wind) and
can lead to sticky discharge or a painful infected swelling beside the nose
(acute dacryocystitis — needs urgent treatment). Adult persistent blockage
is cured by **DCR surgery**, which creates a new tear-drainage passage into
the nose (external or endoscopic through-the-nose approach — high success,
short daycare procedure). **Babies** with watery, sticky eyes from birth
usually have a membrane-blocked duct: the doctor teaches tear-sac massage,
and most open by the first birthday; a simple probing procedure helps those
that don't. Watering has other causes too (dry-eye reflex watering, lid
laxity, in-growing lashes), which the examination sorts out.

## Eyelid position problems and skin growths

With age the lid can roll INWARD (entropion — lashes rub the eye: gritty,
red, watering) or OUTWARD (ectropion — exposed, watering eye); both are
fixed with short corrective surgeries and should not be left to scar the
cornea. In-growing lashes can be removed or permanently treated. Lid skin
growths are usually benign (tags, cysts, xanthelasma — cholesterol patches)
but any lid lesion that grows, bleeds, ulcerates or keeps losing lashes
needs a biopsy to rule out skin cancer — lid cancers caught early are
highly curable with surgery that preserves the lid's function.

## Thyroid eye disease, socket surgery and artificial eyes

**Thyroid eye disease** causes staring/bulging eyes, lid retraction,
grittiness, double vision and, rarely, pressure on the optic nerve — it
needs joint care with the endocrinologist, strict no-smoking (smoking
worsens it markedly), lubricants, and staged treatment; sudden vision drop
or colours fading in a thyroid patient is an emergency. When an eye is
lost to injury, cancer or a blind painful state, socket surgery with an
implant followed by a **custom artificial eye (prosthesis)** restores a
natural appearance — modern prostheses match the other eye closely and are
fitted and polished periodically. Children who lose an eye need prompt
prosthesis fitting so the growing socket develops normally.
