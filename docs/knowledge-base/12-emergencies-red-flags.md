# Eye Emergencies — When to Come NOW

## Summary for prompt

Some eye symptoms need SAME-DAY care because hours decide the outcome. The
bot must recognise these and respond with urgency (come now / nearest
emergency eye care), not with routine booking: sudden loss or blackout of
vision; a curtain/shadow spreading across vision; sudden shower of floaters
with flashes; severe eye pain with redness, halos and vomiting; chemical
splash (rinse with clean water 15–30 minutes FIRST, then come); any
penetrating injury or object stuck in the eye (do NOT remove, do NOT press,
shield and come); post-operative pain/vision drop/discharge; sudden double
vision or droopy lid; painful red eye in a contact-lens user; and white
pupil or injury in a child. When in doubt between "urgent" and "routine",
the bot should choose urgent and offer the earliest visit.

## Sudden painless loss of vision

Vision suddenly going dark, grey or blank in one eye — even if it recovers
in minutes — is an emergency. Causes include retinal artery occlusion (an
"eye stroke", where minutes-to-hours matter), retinal detachment, bleeding
inside the eye, and optic-nerve events. A transient blackout that recovered
(amaurosis fugax) can be a warning sign of stroke risk and still needs
same-day assessment. The bot should never book these as routine
appointments — direct the patient to come immediately.

## Flashes, floaters and curtain — retinal tear/detachment

A sudden burst of new floaters, repeated lightning-like flashes, or a dark
curtain/shadow creeping across the vision = possible retinal tear or
detachment. A tear caught early is sealed by laser in minutes; a detachment
needs surgery, and results are far better when the centre of vision is
still attached. Message: come TODAY, don't wait for the weekend.

## Chemical injury — the only "treat first, travel second" emergency

For any chemical in the eye — acid, alkali, lime/chuna (worst), cleaning
agents, sanitiser, cement, whitewash: IMMEDIATELY wash the open eye with a
steady stream of clean water for 15–30 minutes (remove contact lenses;
hold lids open; any clean water is fine — do not search for special
solutions, do not apply anything else). THEN come urgently. The first
minutes of irrigation matter more than anything the hospital can do later.
For lime/chuna particles, flick out visible solid bits while rinsing.

## Injuries: blunt, sharp and stuck objects

- **Blunt injury** (fist, ball, cork): pain, swelling, bleeding or blurred
  vision after a hit needs same-day examination — bleeding inside the front
  of the eye, lens damage and retinal injury are common and treatable.
- **Sharp/penetrating injury or a stuck object** (wire, knife, glass, metal
  shard): do NOT remove the object, do NOT press, pad or rub; shield the
  eye (bottom of a paper cup taped over it works), keep the patient
  fasting, and come immediately.
- **Grinding/hammering metal particle**: high-speed fragments can enter
  the eye with little pain — any such history after metalwork needs
  examination and often an X-ray/scan.
- **Welding flash / "arc eye"**: intense pain and watering hours after
  welding without goggles — very painful but usually heals in 1–2 days with
  treatment; still needs a check to rule out a burn or foreign body.

## Painful red eye — the dangerous versions

A red eye is urgent when ANY of these accompany it: significant PAIN (not
just irritation), light-sensitivity, drop in vision, coloured halos around
lights (possible acute glaucoma — often with headache/vomiting), a white
spot on the cornea (ulcer), contact-lens wear, recent eye surgery or
injection, or a known uveitis history. These need same-day care. A simple
sticky "eye-flu" red eye without those features can be managed with
hygiene and an early routine visit.

## After eye surgery or injection

Any operated/injected eye that develops INCREASING pain, WORSENING redness,
DROPPING vision or discharge — especially in the first days — must be seen
the same day; post-operative infection is rare but sight-threatening, and
hours matter. This overrides all routine scheduling; the bot should treat
"I had surgery recently and now…" messages as top priority and alert the
staff (handoff) in addition to advising immediate visit.

## Children and one-eyed patients — lower the threshold

For children (who under-report symptoms), patients with only one seeing
eye, and elderly patients living alone, treat borderline symptoms as
urgent. A child's white pupil in photos, an infant avoiding light with
watering, any eye injury in a child, or ANY new symptom in a person's only
good eye — earliest possible visit.

## What the bot should say in an emergency

1. State clearly this needs urgent care today — do not offer a routine slot.
2. For chemicals: give the rinse-first instruction before anything else.
3. Share the hospital's address/directions and (if configured) phone.
4. If the hospital is closed or far, advise the nearest emergency eye
   service rather than waiting.
5. Create a handoff so staff see the conversation immediately.
6. Do not attempt diagnosis, reassurance, or medicine advice in these
   situations.

---
**Safety note for the assistant:** When symptoms match this document, the
assistant must prioritise urgency over booking convenience, must not
downplay symptoms, and must hand off to staff. It is always acceptable to
over-triage; it is never acceptable to under-triage.
