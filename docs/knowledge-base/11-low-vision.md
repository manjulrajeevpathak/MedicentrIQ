# Low Vision & Vision Rehabilitation

## Summary for prompt

Low vision means significant sight loss that glasses, medicines or surgery
cannot fully fix — from advanced ARMD, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy,
inherited retinal disease and other causes. "Nothing more can be done for
the disease" is never the end of the road: low-vision rehabilitation helps
people USE their remaining vision better — magnifiers, telescopic and
high-power reading aids, electronic/digital magnification, lighting and
contrast strategies, smartphone accessibility features, and orientation/
mobility training. Children with low vision need early rehabilitation and
school support so education continues. Eligible patients can also be
guided about disability certification and support schemes. If a patient or
family says "doctors said nothing can be done", offer a low-vision
assessment.

## What a low-vision assessment does

A low-vision assessment measures what the person CAN see — remaining
central sharpness, field of vision, contrast and glare sensitivity — and
matches tasks the person cares about (reading, recognising faces, phone
use, moving about, studying, sewing, shop work) to the right aids and
training. It is practical and goal-driven: the output is a working toolkit
and technique, not just a prescription. Family members are involved,
because home lighting, contrast and habits multiply the benefit of every
aid.

## Aids and strategies that help

- **Optical aids:** high-power reading glasses, hand and stand magnifiers,
  pocket telescopes for boards/signs/bus numbers.
- **Electronic aids:** video magnifiers (CCTV), and the free accessibility
  built into every smartphone — zoom gestures, large bold text, high
  contrast, screen readers (TalkBack/VoiceOver), voice assistants, and
  magnifier camera apps. Teaching a patient three phone settings often
  helps more than any single gadget.
- **Environment:** brighter task lighting from behind the shoulder, bold
  felt-tip pens, black-on-white large print, contrasting tape on stair
  edges and switches, reduced glare (caps/tinted glasses outdoors).
- **Techniques:** eccentric viewing training for central (macular) loss —
  learning to use a healthier off-centre retinal spot; steady-eye reading
  strategies; orientation & mobility training (safe independent movement,
  cane skills) where field loss is severe.

## Children with low vision

A child with low vision needs rehabilitation running in PARALLEL with
medical care — not after it: early-intervention stimulation for babies,
appropriate aids for school (front-row seating, large print, extra time,
digital textbooks), and coordination with educators. With the right
support, children with low vision study, appear for board exams (with
permitted accommodations like scribes/extra time) and build careers. The
earlier the support starts, the better the developmental outcome.

## Certification and support

Patients whose best-corrected vision or visual field meets the legal
criteria can obtain a **disability certificate**, which unlocks government
schemes, travel and tax concessions, educational accommodations and
assistive-device programmes. The eye department guides eligible patients
through the assessment and paperwork, and can connect families to
rehabilitation organisations and support groups. Emotional adjustment is
real work too — counselling and peer groups help patients and caregivers
alike.

---
**Safety note for the assistant:** This content is general education. Frame
low-vision care positively and practically; never tell a patient "nothing
can be done". If vision has dropped RECENTLY or suddenly, that is a medical
evaluation first — rehabilitation follows once the disease is treated and
stable.
